Each ring = 2 kn of boat speed. The dot is where you are now.

What the curve says

  • Top notch the red wedge is the no-go zone — no forward drive exists there.
  • Widest the boat is quickest on a reach (wind across the beam), not downwind.
  • Bottom dead downwind is slow — the sail is a parachute, dragging, not lifting.

Apparent wind

The wind the sail actually feels: AW = TW − V_boat. Speed up and it swings toward the bow. The sail is always trimmed to this, never the true wind.

Lift

Perpendicular to the apparent wind — the wing force. Peaks around 20° angle of attack, collapses if the sail stalls or luffs.

Drag

Along the apparent wind. Small when flow is clean; huge when the sail is broadside — that's what pushes you dead downwind.

Drive + Side

Lift and drag re-summed into the boat's axes: drive pushes you forward, side force makes you heel and slip. Their balance is the whole game.

The core equations (straight from the model)

  • Apparent AW = TW − V_boat — vector subtraction, recomputed every frame.
  • Angle of attack AoA = |AWA| − boom, where boom = min(sheet, |AWA|).
  • Drive q·(C_L·sin|AWA| − C_D·cos|AWA|) — goes negative near head-to-wind → the no-go zone, for free.
  • Side q·(C_L·cos|AWA| + …) — becomes heel and leeway; too much heel spills drive back.
  • Balance drive = hull_drag · v² — solved each frame for the speed you see.
